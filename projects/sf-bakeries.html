Overview

Community, one loaf
of bread at a time.

As part of the Bay Area Immersion Program, I partnered with two fellow students to document the cultural, economic, and emotional stories behind SF's post-pandemic bakery boom. Over 6 weeks, we built a fully original storytelling site from scratch — including concept, reporting, photography, illustrations, UX design, interviews, and final production.

"What does it mean to rebuild community one loaf of bread at a time?"

We interviewed bakery owners, visited their shops in person, and created an immersive digital story with original animations, visual identity, and longform narrative — designed and coded entirely from scratch.

Process

From street to screen.

01
On-Site Reporting & Photography

We visited SF bakeries in person — interviewing owners about their experiences navigating the pandemic, the city's shifting demographics, and what "community" actually means when your shop is the neighborhood's living room. I handled photography and color grading, developing a consistent warm, intimate visual tone that runs throughout the story.

Original photography from our SF bakery visits

02
Illustration & Visual Identity

We designed and illustrated a full suite of original icons and storefront illustrations — giving the digital story a handcrafted, editorial feel that matched the warmth of the subject matter. Every visual element was created from scratch to reinforce the story's emotional tone.

Original storefront illustrations and icon system

03
Design, Code & Production

We designed and coded the full storytelling site from scratch in HTML/CSS — including scroll animations, typographic hierarchy, photo essay layouts, and responsive breakpoints. Every detail was intentional: from the delicate color palette to the hand-coded interactions that guide readers through the narrative.

The result was an immersive digital experience that felt like editorial journalism rather than a student project.

Reflections

What I learned.

This was one of the most creatively fulfilling projects I've worked on. I was able to merge design, storytelling, code, photography, and community listening into a single immersive experience. It taught me the power of food as a lens for journalism — and how deeply design can shape empathy.

We didn't just tell a story. We created an environment to experience one. I left this project more confident in my creative instincts, collaboration skills, and ability to bring ambitious ideas to life from scratch.
