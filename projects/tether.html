Overview

Good design starts
on the trail.

Tether is a conceptual mobile app + physical product designed to help hikers find community while exploring trails. Created as part of UX Design Studio (DSGN 306) during the Bay Area Immersion in San Francisco, this end-to-end design sprint was grounded in field research, on-trail testing, and iterative storytelling.

The core insight: hiking is inherently social, but existing tools treat it as a solo, transactional experience. Tether bridges the gap between digital planning and physical connection — letting hikers find each other, share routes, and build real community around trails.

"Our best insights came from asking questions, listening deeply, and walking the paths our users walk."

Process

From field research
to Figma.

01
Field Research & User Interviews

We went where our users were — conducting field research on actual trails, observing hiking behavior, and interviewing hikers about how they plan routes, find companions, and experience safety and community on the trail. We talked to solo hikers, group hikers, and trail regulars to build a nuanced picture of the problem space.

Key tension we uncovered: Hikers craved spontaneous social connection but had no low-friction way to find it. Every existing tool (AllTrails, Meetup, Facebook groups) required too much pre-planning and too much commitment.

On-trail field research and synthesis sessions

02
Prototyping — Paper to Digital

We prototyped in rapid cycles — starting with paper sketches to test flows without commitment, then building mid-fidelity Figma screens as our concepts sharpened. We tested with other hikers throughout, each round surfacing new friction points and new opportunities.

The physical component: We designed a companion physical device — a trail marker beacon that lets hikers signal their location and availability for connection without needing to pull out a phone on a steep trail.

03
Final Design & Showcase

The final Tether design features a clean, trail-specific visual language — earthy tones, generous whitespace, and a map-first interface that puts trail information and nearby hikers front and center. We presented at the Bay Area final showcase with a live interactive demo.

Reflections

What I learned.

This project reminded me that good design starts on the trail — not in the studio. Our best insights came from asking questions, listening deeply, and walking the paths our users walk. I loved blending digital and physical storytelling to create a product that encourages connection, curiosity, and confidence in the outdoors.
