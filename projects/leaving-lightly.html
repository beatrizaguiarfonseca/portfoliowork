Overview

Turning move-out chaos
into circular community.

Every spring, Northwestern students dump tons of usable furniture. Seniors need to move out fast. Rising juniors want furniture — but can't store it over summer. The gap between "I don't need this anymore" and "I would love that" was costing the planet 230 tons of waste per year, per college.

Our team researched the full move-out experience, mapped barriers between intention and action, and prototyped a digital platform that connects students through verified listings, timing-sensitive logistics, and real incentives to participate.

"How might we reduce college move-out waste by facilitating sustainable and convenient item exchanges between students?"

Research Insights

What we found.

230T
Furniture waste generated annually at the average U.S. college move-out — most of it still perfectly usable.
83%
Items still go to waste even when donation options exist. The problem isn't awareness — it's friction.
Time
Students feel overwhelmed by time pressure and emotional stress during move-out, making sustainable choices harder.
Gap
SustainNU and similar programs are severely underfunded — willing participants, no reliable infrastructure.
Process

Research, prototype, repeat.

01
Empathy Mapping & User Research

Who we talked to: Graduating seniors stressed about packing timelines, incoming students anxious about furnishing dorms on a budget, parents navigating logistics from afar, and sustainability coordinators working with almost no resources.

Key tension: Everyone wanted to do the right thing. Nobody had the time or coordination infrastructure to actually do it. Our solution needed to remove friction, not add more.

Empathy mapping and user research — understanding the full move-out emotional journey

02
Ideation & Concept Development

From app wireframes to pop-up furniture swaps, we tested multiple directions — exploring both digital and physical interactions to reduce barriers at every stage of move-out. We ran three ideation sprints and narrowed to a single focused direction: a digital platform with timing-sensitive verified listings, a points-based reward system, and university-partnered pickup windows.

Core insight: The solution couldn't ask more of stressed students. It had to do the work for them — surfacing the right items, at the right time, to the right people.

03
Prototyping, Testing & Iteration

We built three rounds of prototypes — from rough paper sketches to mid-fidelity flows to a polished Figma prototype — testing at each stage with real Northwestern students. Key insights shaped the final flow: users wanted fewer screens, clearer timing cues, and seller-first onboarding that got items listed in under 60 seconds.

User testing feedback and data synthesis

04
Final Prototype & Presentation

The final Figma prototype demonstrates the full student flow — from listing a couch in under a minute, to browsing nearby available items, to arranging pickup within a university-coordinated window.

Interactive Figma prototype — student-verified secondhand furniture exchange

Final presentation deck — from problem to prototype

Reflections

What I learned.

This project challenged me to think beyond screens and into real-world behavior, habits, and emotions. Our solution wasn't just about an app — it was about understanding what stands in the way of sustainable action and designing something that bridges that gap.

The most important insight: people want to do the right thing. Good design doesn't convince them to care — it removes the obstacles between caring and acting. That's the job.
