Overview

Bridging digital listening
with physical fandom.

As part of Northwestern's Bay Area Immersion Program hosted by the Segal Design Institute, our team partnered with Amazon Music to explore how physical products, leveraging Amazon's unrivaled logistics, could turn passive listeners into emotionally connected fans.

Amazon Music has an enormous user base but sits in the shadow of Spotify's cultural dominance. The challenge wasn't the product, it was identity and emotional resonance. We set out to design experiences that made Amazon Music feel like more than a streaming service.

"How might we leverage Amazon's expertise in physical products to make Amazon Music a more recognizable and emotionally meaningful part of the Amazon brand?"

We presented the full system in person at Amazon Music's San Francisco headquarters and the response was everything.

"I love all of these ideas! I feel there is a lot of potential in this and I'm especially impressed and curious about the yearly recap posters."

— Ebube, Amazon Music Product Manager
Process

From insight to prototype.

01
Research & Discovery

User Interviews: We spoke with Gen Z and Millennial listeners about how they discover, share, and emotionally relate to music, and what they wish streaming services understood about them.

Competitive Benchmarking: We mapped Spotify's cultural dominance (Wrapped, Blend, artist merch) and identified Amazon's unique structural advantages: Prime ecosystem, one-day shipping, and massive marketplace reach.

Key Insight: Listeners don't just consume music, they use it to express identity. Amazon was uniquely positioned to make that expression tangible and physical.

Workshop sketches and ideation sessions at the Segal Design Institute

02
Ideation — Four Concepts

We ran three HMW sessions and converged on four distinct concepts, each targeting a different aspect of fan identity and Amazon's platform strengths.

🖼️
Amazon Music Playback Poster
A personalized, auto-generated print poster mailed to users who streamed 10,000+ minutes in a year. A physical Spotify Wrapped, tangible, displayable.
🏠
Design My Music Room
A decor tool that reads your top artists and generates shoppable room aesthetic suggestions (posters, merch, furniture) powered by Amazon Marketplace inventory.
🎁
Prime Advent Calendar
A seasonal surprise box filled with exclusive merch, vinyl, and artist collaborations, an Amazon Prime Music perk that deepens loyalty and creates shareable moments.
🎧
Virtual Listening Party
A real-time synchronized listening room where fans can react and connect while streaming the same song, turning a solitary experience into a social one.
03
The Playback Poster: Hero Deliverable

The Amazon Music Playback Poster generated the most excitement from Amazon's product team. I designed all three versions myself in Illustrator and Figma, each fully personalized with artist, listening stats, key tracks, and a unique visual identity.

The positioning was intentional: Amazon already ships things to your door. This was simply making fandom shippable.

Three custom Playback Posters: Taylor Swift, Kendrick Lamar, and Hozier

04
Prototyping & Final Pitch

We built three interactive Figma prototypes to demonstrate real user flows for the Amazon Music product team, then presented the full suite in person at Amazon Music HQ in San Francisco.

Reflections

What I learned.

This project fundamentally changed how I think about digital products. Designing for Amazon's ecosystem meant thinking far beyond screens, into cardboard, ink, logistics, nostalgia, and the social psychology of sharing your taste with the world.

The Playback Poster taught me something specific: the most resonant products make invisible things visible. Streaming is invisible. A poster on your wall is not. Great design finds the bridge between the two.

Presenting to an Amazon PM in person also sharpened my ability to defend creative choices with both emotional and business logic simultaneously.
